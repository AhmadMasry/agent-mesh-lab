THE THREE DECISIONS THIS STEP TOOK, each recorded before the first counted repetition
  1. ONE RECEIVER, the Go receiver. The brief's grid is proxy x method x 5 and names no receiver, and by the
     author's note of 2026-09-20 the streaming client is the load client (a2a-go) in every B row, so the client SDK
     does not vary either. The Go receiver was chosen because on B-4's measured paths it puts each proxy on exactly
     ONE leg of a repetition, which is what makes each removal attributable: the agentgateway ingress carries the
     A2A stream (B-4 counted 80 of 80 arrivals from the ingress pod and 0 from agw-central for its Go half) and
     agw-central carries the model call and no A2A line at all. The Python receiver's card GET crosses agw-central
     as well, which would put both proxies inside one repetition. B-5b runs both receivers.
  2. N = 45 000 ms, unchanged from B-3 and B-4. It is the only N this lab has MEASURED the path to carry with no
     timeout set anywhere (the B-3 entry of 2026-09-22: "No N above 45 s was tried, and the 60 s ceilings say where
     the next limit is"), and keeping it makes this step's stimulus comparable with the B-4 control that is set
     beside it. The proxy is removed 2 000 ms after the client's own TASK_STATE_WORKING event, so the stream has
     about 43 s of life left at the moment of the removal. The consequence is stated rather than discovered: 43 s is
     under 60 s, so a cut that falls at the pod's grace CANNOT be seen at the client at any N under the callers'
     60 s ceilings, and the brief forbids raising a ceiling. In such a repetition the discriminator is the removed
     pod's own drain log and how long the pod actually lived, both of which this step captures.
  3. THE PROBE for "seconds until the successor serves" is two readings, not one: the successor's Ready condition
     from the Kubernetes API, which needs no request at all, and ONE loadgen Job of the row's own kind (MODE=stream,
     nothing armed on the mock, so its model call answers at once) applied the moment the successor is first seen
     Ready -- one send, no retry, never repeated, and a refusal would be a recorded outcome. It goes out while
     Job 1's stream may still be open, deliberately: waiting for Job 1 would add the old pod's whole drain to a
     figure that is about the successor.

THE DRY REPETITIONS, AND THE ONE THING THEY CHANGED
  dry/ingress-graceful/b5a-ingress-graceful-d1-01 (19:26:39Z-19:27:33Z) was the first. The stimulus worked and the
  collection did not, and both are kept as they stand. `make ledgers` ran 13 s BEFORE the mock's invocation line and
  the executor's terminal state line existed, because a stream can end long before the Task and the model call do --
  which is exactly what B-4's control established. That repetition therefore has invocations=0 and a final state of
  TASK_STATE_WORKING, and neither figure is a reading about the proxy. rows.sh gained wait_settled for it: after both
  Jobs end it reads the worker's and the mock's pod logs (it sends nothing) and waits for the executor's terminal
  state line, and then for the mock's invocation line until the arm stamp plus N plus 30 s at the latest, with a
  180 s ceiling on the whole wait.
  dry/central-forced/b5a-central-forced-d2-01 (19:30:31Z-19:30:57Z) was the second, on the other proxy and the
  method B-5b will use, and it proved the fix: it settled in 0.0 s with the terminal state and the invocation
  outcome both already present.

THE TWO ITEMS CARRIED FROM THE B-4 REVIEW
  1. internal/httpclient.New leaves CheckRedirect at Go's default, so up to ten 3xx answers would be FOLLOWED and
     CLIENT_HOST re-applied to each, and no test pins "a 3xx is not followed" at this tree. A removed proxy is when a
     gateway is likeliest to answer non-2xx, so every repetition records what its client actually saw -- http_status,
     content_type, the first JSON-RPC error object on the wire, the SDK's error text -- and the POST round trips the
     client's own outermost transport carried, which a followed redirect would increase; and counts.py reports every
     non-2xx and every 3xx line in either proxy's access log. NO CODE WAS CHANGED for this in B-5a. The findings
     entry states that no redirect policy is pinned at this tree.
  2. canceller.stop() (fixtures/loadgen/cancel.go) has a microsecond-wide race behind `ended_before_cancel`: the
     canceller's timer and the reader's end can both reach it. It cannot matter in B-5a, where CANCEL_AFTER_MS is
     empty in every Job and nothing cancels. It is written here for B-5b, which cancels nothing either by the brief
     but whose Job 2 arrives while a stream is ending, and for whoever next changes that file.

  WHAT WAS NOT PREDICTED, and is recorded rather than smoothed:
  - the two proxies differ in WHAT a removal costs, and this step's one-receiver choice is what makes that legible.

    invocation, outcome ok, 45 000.4-45 004.6 ms. Removing agw-central cost the Task everything: TASK_STATE_FAILED in

  - the mock's own line says which side went first: outcome `client-gone` in 15 of 15 agw-central repetitions, with
    the delay abandoned at 4 155-4 211 ms (forced), 12 130-12 190 ms (graceful) and 13 577-14 435 ms (rollout) of its
    45 000 ms, each about the moment the proxy died.
  - the rollout against 6.4's derived maxSurge reading, in two halves. SHOWN: at the moment the rollout command
    returned the old pod carried no deletionTimestamp at all, 5 of 5 per rollout variant against one present in 5 of
    5 for a graceful delete, so it had not been asked to stop; its SIGTERM then came 1 463.8-2 376.2 ms after the
    command. NOT shown by this design, and this file claimed it until the review: the successor's Ready
    lastTransitionTime is at or before that SIGTERM 10 of 10, but it is an API stamp at one-second resolution and
    the margin is 114.9-989.2 ms in every one of the ten, so the true transition could fall either side and the pair
    resolves the order in none. counts.py now carries that margin and says so in its own column

  - an operator's record of the request a removal cut is incomplete in a way that differs by proxy and method. The

  - the span processor's 5 s shutdown timeout stands between SIGTERM and the drain in 2 of 32 removals -- dry 1 and
