B-6 — what each collected trace shows, and what it does not
============================================================

Twelve traces were exported with `make export-trace` (LOOKBACK=86400), one directory each under traces/, each
naming the repetition it is. trace-records.txt is the mechanical record of all twelve: span count, trace ids,
roots, dangling parents, every span as a tree under its parent with kind, offset, duration, HTTP status,
agentgateway route and OpenTelemetry status, and the GenAI attributes on the chat span. dangling.csv and genai/
are follow-ups 14's own dangling.py and genai-spans.py run UNEDITED over that directory. trace-census.csv is the
same reading taken over ALL 150 counted work items of B-4, B-5a and B-5b, so that what one trace shows is stated
as a count.

Three rows have no trace, and they are gone WITH their cluster, not evicted from a live one
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The three are B-3's CLEAN STREAM, B-3's SUBSCRIBETOTASK DURING A RUNNING TASK, and D3's SUBSCRIBETOTASK ON A
TERMINAL TASK. The distinction matters to anyone re-running this lab, so it is stated rather than implied:
nothing expired and no retention setting is involved. The backend those three ran against was DELETED by B-4's
rebuild, and the Jaeger pod now standing started 2026-09-22T17:05:20Z (cluster-unchanged.txt), after all three.
A trace backend that was torn down holds nothing.

Every counted work item of every B row was probed rather than a sample -- 270 in all, one query each through the
same binding `make export-trace` uses (backend-inventory.txt): B-3 0 OF 120, which is 0 of 40 on each of the
three rows, against B-4 40 of 40, B-5a 30 of 30 and B-5b 80 of 80. NO ROW WAS RE-RUN TO MAKE A TRACE.

The shape, and where the two SDKs differ in it
-----------------------------------------------
Every work item is FOUR traces, not one, at both receivers: the card GET and the POST of Job 1, then the card GET
and the POST of Job 2. Each has its own root at the load client. They are tied together only by `lab.work_item`,
an attribute this lab puts on its own spans and on the receivers' server spans -- not by a parent, not by a span
link, and not by anything either SDK, either proxy or the conventions supply. The B-5a variants have two traces,
having no Job 2.

Go work items are 18-22 spans; Python ones are 93-101, because the Python image's zero-code instrumentation emits
the a2a-sdk's own internals (EventQueueSource, JsonRpcDispatcher, DefaultRequestHandlerV2). Those carry OK
statuses, not errors.

1. The moment the stream ends
------------------------------
The client's own root span carries it, and nothing else does.

  B-4 control, go and py       `otel=ERROR "SSE stream error: context canceled"`, 40 of 40, at 5000.2-5003.5 ms
  every ingress removal        `otel=ERROR "SSE stream error: unexpected EOF"` -- B-5b 40 of 40, B-5a 15 of 15
  every agw-central removal    `otel=UNSET`, NO error at all -- B-5b 40 of 40, B-5a 15 of 15

The third line is the point of B-5b's companion row put in one cell: on the proxy that carries the model leg the
stream never broke, it carried TASK_STATE_FAILED and ended cleanly, and the trace says so by having nothing to
say. A reader of the trace alone would see a successful stream there.

What the trace does NOT show at that moment: the two ledgers' `client-gone` and `consumer-gone` appear in no
span, on either receiver, in any of the twelve. The proxy's SERVER span for the cut POST, where it exists at all,
reads `http.status=200` -- the same reading as its access line, which B-5a and B-5b already counted.

  B-5a ingress FORCED                agentgateway-ingress contributes NO span at all; both of the worker's
                                     SERVER spans are orphaned -- 2 dangling, 5 of 5
  B-5a ingress graceful / rollout    the card-GET pair survives, the in-flight POST pair does not -- 1 dangling
                                     (worker POST /), 5 of 5 each
  B-5a / B-5b agw-central            both model-leg spans lost with the pod, the mock's SERVER span orphaned --
                                     1 dangling, 55 of 55 across the two steps
  B-5b go ingress-forced             2 dangling in 17 of 20 and 1 in 3 -- in those three the removed pod's batch

  B-5b py ingress-forced             1 dangling (orchestrator POST /), 20 of 20: on that path the card GET

In three of B-5b's go ingress-forced repetitions the count is 1 rather than 2, and the three are named:
b5b-go-ingress-forced-r1-06, -r1-07 and -r1-08, which carry 20 spans where the other seventeen carry 18. In
those three the removed pod's batch holding the card GET had already reached the collector when it died.

THIS IS NOT A REGRESSION AGAINST THE STANDARD PROOF, AND THE TWO ARE DIFFERENT QUESTIONS. Every rebuild proof in
this repository counts 0 dangling parents per work item, and every one of those clean checks is taken with NO
PROXY REMOVED -- which is exactly what B-4's control reproduces here, 0 in 40 of 40. A dangling parent in the
removal rows is the removed pod's UNEXPORTED BATCH, so the absence IS the removal, which is this section's own
heading read from the other side. A span is exported when it ENDS; a span that ends at the pod's death dies with
it, and a forced delete leaves nothing at all. The proof asks "did every hop report?", and the answer there is
still yes. This asks "what did the pod that was killed manage to report?", and the answer is the finding.

3. The moment the resubscription arrives
-----------------------------------------
Almost nothing -- and the sentence has to be exact, because a configuration for half of it EXISTS and this lab
did not use it. Job 2 is its own root in its own trace, 120 of 120 -- no parent, no span link, no reference to
Job 1. Every attribute on its four spans was read (b5b-go-ingress-forced, traces/): the proxy's SERVER span
carries `route=lab/worker-ingress`, `http.method=POST`, `http.path=/`, `http.status=200`, `http.host`,
`duration`, the peer address and its own trace and span ids; the receiver's server span carries `lab.work_item`
and `lab.caller`. Job 2's proxy span is `agentgateway-ingress POST /*` in 120 of 120, on `lab/worker-ingress`
for the Go rows and `lab/orchestrator-ingress` for the Python ones.

STATED AS MEASURED, over all 423 SPANS of the twelve traces: AT THIS CONFIGURATION NO *ATTRIBUTE* NAMES THE A2A
OPERATION AND NO *PROXY* SPAN DOES; THE OPERATION IS NAMED ONLY IN THE PYTHON RECEIVER'S OWN SPAN NAMES, AND
NOWHERE AT ALL ON THE GO PATH; AND NO A2A HOP CARRIES THE TASK ID. The parts have different causes, they are
named separately, none is a project gap, and the middle one is a third SDK asymmetry counted here.

  (a0) NO ATTRIBUTE NAMES THE OPERATION, on any span of any hop. Across the 423 spans, 0 attribute keys match
      `a2a.*` or `rpc.*`; the only protocol-shaped attributes are `gen_ai.agent.name|version|description` on
      the `invoke_agent` span, one per trace. The only keys whose text contains `a2a` at all are
      `http.request.header.x_a2a_message_id` and `http.request.header.x_a2a_task_id`, on 3 spans, each the
      Python receiver's MODEL-LEG client span -- not an A2A hop.

  (a) NO PROXY SPAN NAMES IT EITHER, AND THAT IS THIS LAB'S ROUTES, not a limit of agentgateway. The 72 proxy
      spans carry seven distinct names between them, all `POST /*` or `POST <service>:8080`, with route, host,
      path, method, status and duration and nothing of the body.
      agentgateway v1.5.0 reads the JSON-RPC `method` into its logs and spans FOR A BACKEND MARKED A2A -- the
      author's note of 2026-09-20 in docs/proposal-notes.md ("Experiment C on the agentgateway-only topology")
      states the capability, and findings.md "Experiment C / both receivers / observation" names those `a2a.*`
      span and log attributes as present in agentgateway's source and absent from its published attribute
      reference (its follow-up (a)). The module was never switched on here: it keys on the Service
      `appProtocol` marking, absent on 3 of 3 `lab` Services -- counted by that C entry and RE-READ on this
      standing cluster at 2026-09-22T23:44:20Z for this step (a2a-marking.txt: `appProtocol` unset on worker,
      orchestrator and mockllm, 0 of 3 carrying one) -- and `"a2a"` occurs 0 times in either proxy's
      /config_dump, which that entry counted at these same versions and this step did not re-read. With the
      JSON-RPC binding the operation is a field in the body and every operation shares one path, so with the
      module off a proxy has nothing to read it from.

  (a1) BUT ONE LAYER DOES NAME THE OPERATION, AND IT IS THE PYTHON RECEIVER'S OWN INSTRUMENTATION -- A THIRD
      SDK ASYMMETRY, COUNTED HERE. The zero-code instrumentation in the orchestrator's image emits spans from
      scope `a2a-python-sdk` named after the handler method it is running:
        a2a.server.request_handlers.default_request_handler_v2.DefaultRequestHandlerV2.on_subscribe_to_task
      on Job 2's trace, and ...on_message_send_stream on Job 1's, each descending from the ingress SERVER span
      through `orchestrator POST /` and the JSON-RPC dispatcher. Present in 3 OF THE 3 PYTHON WORK ITEMS among
      the twelve and in 0 OF THE 9 GO ONES: the Go receiver's 42 spans carry four distinct names --
      `GET /.well-known/agent-card.json`, `POST /`, `HTTP POST`, `chat mock` -- and name no operation anywhere.
      So the A2A operation is VISIBLE IN THE PYTHON RECEIVER'S SPAN NAMES AND NOWHERE ON THE GO PATH: a
      difference in what each SDK's telemetry NAMES, beside the two differences in what each SDK's LEDGER
      records. The distinction between a span NAME and a span ATTRIBUTE is one this lab had already drawn and
      the first version of this file collapsed -- findings.md, "Experiment C / both receivers / observation",
      records that the `a2a.` strings in the orchestrator's trace are span names the Python SDK emits in the
      application, not proxy attributes. How the slip happened is visible two paragraphs above, where this file
      says "Every attribute on its four spans was read (b5b-go-ingress-forced, traces/)": the Go Job 2 trace has
      FOUR spans and the Python ones have sixteen to nineteen, so a reading taken on the Go trace was
      generalised to all twelve. It was caught by the B-6 review, which parsed all twelve.

  (b) THE TASK ID IS MISSING BECAUSE OF THE SDKs AND OF THIS LAB'S OWN INSTRUMENTATION. Neither a2a-go v2.5.0
      nor a2a-python 1.1.4 writes an `a2a.*` or `rpc.*` attribute of its own: across all twelve traces the only
      protocol-shaped attributes that exist are `gen_ai.agent.name|version|description`, on the `invoke_agent`
      span, one per trace. `lab.task_id` is this lab's own attribute and is set only where a task id is in hand
      at the model leg -- the mock's server span (12 of 12 traces), the Go `chat mock` span (9 of 9 Go traces)
      and the Python httpx `POST` span (3 of 3 Python traces) -- and on NO A2A hop in any of the twelve.

So the reattachment that B's whole question turns on is invisible in the trace, and it is the TASK ID that
decides this rather than the operation: at the Python receiver a trace can say THAT a resubscription arrived,
from that handler-method span name, and at the Go receiver not even that -- but on neither path can it say WHAT
it reattached to, no A2A hop carrying a task id in any form. The results table's "what the one
`SubscribeToTask` reattached to" column comes from the pre-dispatch ingress ledger, the execution ledger and the
client's own lines, and COULD NOT have come from the traces at this configuration. That is §4.3's division of labour read back from a run, stated from both directions at once:
ledgers are ground truth, traces explain, and here the explanation stops at the HTTP boundary.

4. The task that outlives its stream
-------------------------------------
This is the one thing the trace shows better than any ledger, and it shows it as a shape: a CHILD span that
outlives its PARENT. On every repetition where the stream was cut and the Task lived -- B-4's 40, B-5b's
ingress-forced 40, B-5a's three ingress variants' 15, 95 in all -- the receiver's `POST /` SERVER span ends with
the stream, 2 120-29 360 ms after the send depending on how the stream was ended, while its own descendant
`chat mock` runs 45 002-45 023 ms. The chat span outlives its parent in 95 of 95, by 15 644-42 884 ms. The
proxy's SERVER span and the client's both end at the same moment as the receiver's, and nothing below `chat
mock` notices that everything above it has ended.
